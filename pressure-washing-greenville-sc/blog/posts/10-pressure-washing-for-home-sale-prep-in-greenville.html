Pressure Washing for Home Sale Prep in Greenville

Selling your Greenville home? Pressure washing is one of the highest-ROI improvements you can make before listing. For $300-$600, you can add thousands to your sale price by making the exterior look clean and well-maintained. Buyers decide how they feel about a house before they walk through the front door.

Why Curb Appeal Matters in Greenville's Market

The Greenville real estate market is competitive. Buyers often drive by a property before scheduling a showing. If the driveway is stained, the siding is green with mildew, or the walkways look neglected, many buyers will keep driving.

According to the National Association of Realtors, curb appeal improvements return 100% or more of their cost at sale. Pressure washing is the fastest and cheapest way to transform your home's exterior appearance in a single day.

What to Pressure Wash Before Listing

Focus on every surface a buyer will see from the street and during a walkthrough:

  • Driveway and garage floor — oil stains, tire marks, and general grime are the first thing buyers notice. A clean driveway signals a well-maintained home.
  • Sidewalks and front walkway — clean paths make the approach to the front door feel welcoming
  • House siding (all sides) — mildew, pollen, and dirt make a house look old. A soft wash brings back the original color.
  • Front porch and steps — the area where buyers stand while waiting for the door to open. It needs to be spotless.
  • Back patio and deck — buyers inspect outdoor living spaces closely. A dirty deck suggests deferred maintenance.
  • Fence — especially if it is visible from the street or the backyard is part of the selling points
  • Gutters (exterior) — black streaks on gutter faces are caused by oxidation and runoff. Cleaning them makes the entire roofline look newer.
  • Mailbox area and curb — small details that add to the overall impression

Cost Breakdown for Pre-Sale Pressure Washing

Here is what a full pre-sale cleaning package typically costs in Greenville:

  • Full house wash (siding, soffits, gutters) — $250-$450
  • Driveway cleaning — $100-$200
  • Sidewalks and walkways — $50-$100
  • Deck or patio wash — $150-$300
  • Fence cleaning — $100-$250
  • Complete package (house + driveway + walkways + patio) — $400-$800

Most Greenville homeowners spend $400-$600 on a full pre-sale wash. On a $350,000 home, that is less than 0.2% of the sale price for a dramatic visual improvement.

When to Schedule: Timing Your Wash for Maximum Impact

Timing matters. Here is the ideal schedule relative to your listing date:

  • 2-3 weeks before listing photos — this gives enough time for the wash, any touch-up painting, and landscaping to look fresh in photos
  • Not more than 4-5 weeks before listing — in Greenville's climate, pollen and mildew can start returning within a month, especially in spring
  • After any exterior repairs — fix damaged siding, replace rotted trim, and recaulk windows first, then wash everything
  • Before landscaping refresh — wash first, then do fresh mulch and plantings so they are not covered in chemical runoff or debris

What Realtors in Greenville Recommend

Local real estate agents consistently list pressure washing as their top pre-sale recommendation. Here is why:

  • It costs less than staging a single room but improves the entire exterior
  • Listing photos look dramatically better — online photos are where 95% of buyers start
  • It removes the "neglected" impression that leads to lowball offers
  • It is one of the few improvements that can be done in a single day with zero disruption

Step-by-Step: What Happens on Service Day

  1. Walk-through — the crew checks all surfaces, notes any damage or areas needing special attention, and confirms the scope of work
  2. Plant and furniture protection — landscaping is pre-soaked, and outdoor items are moved or covered
  3. House wash — siding is soft washed starting from the top down to prevent streaking
  4. Hard surface cleaning — driveway, walkways, and patio are pressure washed with surface cleaners for even results
  5. Detail work — front porch, steps, gutter faces, and other high-visibility areas get extra attention
  6. Final rinse and inspection — everything is rinsed clean and the crew does a final walk to check for missed spots

Frequently Asked Questions

Will pressure washing reveal problems I need to fix before selling?

Sometimes, yes. Removing dirt and mildew can expose wood rot, cracked siding, or peeling paint that was hidden underneath. This is actually a good thing — it is better to find and fix these issues before a buyer's inspector does. Discovering problems during inspection leads to renegotiation or lost deals.

Should I pressure wash if my house is going to be painted?

Absolutely. Pressure washing is required prep before exterior painting. Schedule the wash 2-5 days before the painters arrive. See our guide to washing before painting for full details.

Can I pressure wash my own house before selling?

For the driveway and walkways, a rental machine can work if you are careful. For the house siding, hire a professional. Wrong pressure or technique can damage siding, and the streaky results from amateur work can actually make the house look worse in listing photos. The $300-$500 for professional service is worth it.
