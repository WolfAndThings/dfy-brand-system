Deck Cleaning and Restoration in Greenville

Greenville's heat and humidity are hard on wood decks. Between mildew, UV damage, and foot traffic, most decks in the area need professional cleaning every 1-2 years to stay safe and looking good. Here is what deck restoration involves and what it costs in Greenville.

Why Decks Deteriorate Faster in Greenville

Greenville sits in a humid subtropical climate zone. That means your deck faces:

  • Mold and mildew growth — warm, humid air and afternoon shade create ideal conditions for green and black growth on wood
  • UV damage — South Carolina sun grays and dries out unprotected wood in a single season
  • Pollen buildup — spring pollen settles into wood grain and creates a sticky film that traps moisture
  • Red clay staining — splashback from rain hitting Greenville's red clay soil stains deck boards and footings
  • Leaf stain and tannin bleed — leaves sitting on a wet deck leave dark stains that soak into the wood

What Deck Restoration Includes

A full deck restoration is more than just spraying water. The process typically involves three stages:

  1. Cleaning — a deck-safe cleaning solution is applied to kill mold and mildew and break down dirt. The deck is then washed at low pressure (under 1,500 PSI for most wood species) to avoid damaging the wood fibers.
  2. Brightening — after cleaning, a wood brightener (usually oxalic acid based) is applied to restore the wood's natural color and open the grain for stain absorption.
  3. Sealing or staining — once the deck is fully dry (48-72 hours), a penetrating stain or sealant is applied to protect against moisture and UV. This step is optional but highly recommended.

Cost Breakdown for Deck Cleaning in Greenville

  • Basic deck wash only — $150-$300 for a standard 200-400 sq ft deck
  • Wash + brightening — $250-$450
  • Full restoration (wash + brighten + stain) — $500-$1,200 depending on deck size and stain quality
  • Railing and stair cleaning add-on — $50-$150 extra
  • Board replacement (if needed) — $15-$40 per board, varies by species

Compare that to full deck replacement at $15,000-$30,000+. Regular cleaning and sealing every 1-2 years extends a deck's life by 10-15 years.

What to Expect on Service Day

  1. The crew arrives and moves furniture and planters off the deck
  2. Landscaping below and around the deck is pre-wetted to protect from cleaning solutions
  3. Cleaning solution is applied and allowed to dwell for 5-10 minutes
  4. The deck is washed with controlled low pressure, board by board
  5. Brightener is applied and rinsed
  6. The deck is left to dry for 2-3 days before stain application (if included)
  7. Furniture is moved back after completion

Pressure Wash vs Soft Wash for Decks

Most wood decks should be soft washed or washed at very low pressure. High-pressure washing (above 2,000 PSI) damages wood by:

  • Raising the grain and creating a rough, splintery surface
  • Gouging soft wood like pine and cedar
  • Driving water deep into the wood, extending dry time
  • Creating uneven marks where the spray overlapped

A professional crew uses the right PSI for your wood species and adjusts the tip and distance to clean without damage. This is the main reason DIY deck washing often goes wrong.

When to Schedule Deck Cleaning in Greenville

  • Best months — March through May or September through November. Mild temps and lower humidity allow faster drying.
  • Avoid — mid-summer when heat can cause cleaning solutions to dry too fast, and winter when temperatures drop below 50 degrees (stain will not cure properly).
  • Before a party or listing — schedule at least 1 week before any event to allow full drying and staining time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I clean my deck in Greenville?

Every 1-2 years for cleaning, with re-staining every 2-3 years. Decks in heavy shade or near trees may need annual cleaning due to faster mildew growth.

Can you clean composite decking (Trex, TimberTech)?

Yes. Composite decks still get dirty and grow mold. We use composite-safe cleaning solutions and low pressure. Composite decks do not need staining but benefit from annual washing.

Should I sand my deck instead of washing it?

Sanding removes more material and is rarely necessary if the deck is washed and brightened properly. Sanding makes sense only if the wood has deep damage or you are removing a heavy film-forming stain. For most Greenville decks, washing and brightening is the better approach.
