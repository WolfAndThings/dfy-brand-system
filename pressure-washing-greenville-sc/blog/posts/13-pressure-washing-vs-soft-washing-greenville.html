Pressure Washing vs Soft Washing in Greenville, SC

Greenville homeowners often hear both terms used interchangeably, but pressure washing and soft washing are two fundamentally different cleaning methods. Using the wrong one on the wrong surface can cause costly damage. Here is a clear breakdown of how they differ and when each method is the right call for your property.

What Is Pressure Washing?

Pressure washing uses high-pressure water — typically 2,500 to 4,000 PSI — to blast dirt, grime, oil stains, and organic growth off hard surfaces. The force of the water does the cleaning, not chemicals. It is the go-to method for durable surfaces that can handle the impact.

Best surfaces for pressure washing:

  • Concrete driveways and sidewalks
  • Brick and stone patios
  • Concrete pool decks
  • Parking lots and garage floors
  • Retaining walls (concrete or stone)

What Is Soft Washing?

Soft washing uses low-pressure water — usually under 500 PSI — combined with specialized cleaning solutions (typically sodium hypochlorite-based) to kill algae, mold, mildew, and bacteria at the root. The chemicals do the work, and the low pressure simply rinses the surface clean without risking damage.

Best surfaces for soft washing:

  • Vinyl, aluminum, and wood siding
  • Roof shingles and tiles
  • Painted or stained surfaces
  • Stucco and EIFS
  • Fences and decks (especially older wood)
  • Screens, awnings, and outdoor furniture

Why It Matters in Greenville

Greenville's humid subtropical climate is a breeding ground for algae, mold, and mildew. The combination of warm temperatures, high humidity, and regular rainfall means organic growth shows up on every exterior surface within months of cleaning. This is why soft washing tends to deliver better long-term results in Greenville — it kills the organisms instead of just knocking them off the surface.

If you pressure wash vinyl siding, you may get a clean-looking result for a few weeks. But the algae roots are still embedded in the surface, and regrowth starts almost immediately. Soft washing eliminates that cycle by killing the growth at the cellular level.

Pros and Cons of Pressure Washing

Pros:

  • Extremely effective on concrete, brick, and stone
  • No chemicals needed for most hard-surface jobs
  • Fast results — you can see the surface transform in real time
  • Lower cost per job on average

Cons:

  • Can damage vinyl siding, wood, paint, and roofing
  • Does not kill algae or mold — only removes the visible layer
  • Can force water behind siding and into wall cavities
  • Shorter-lasting results on organic-growth-prone surfaces

Pros and Cons of Soft Washing

Pros:

  • Safe for all exterior surfaces including roofs
  • Kills mold, algae, and bacteria at the root
  • Results last 3-4 times longer than pressure washing alone
  • No risk of water intrusion behind siding

Cons:

  • Costs 15-25% more than standard pressure washing
  • Chemicals require proper handling and plant protection
  • Not effective for removing oil stains or heavy mineral deposits
  • Requires trained applicators for proper dilution ratios

Which Is Right for You?

The answer depends on the surface being cleaned. Most Greenville properties need both methods across different areas. A typical whole-home cleaning job uses pressure washing on the driveway, sidewalks, and patio, then switches to soft washing for the siding, trim, and roof.

Choose pressure washing if: You need to clean concrete, brick, or stone surfaces with heavy staining, oil deposits, or ground-in dirt.

Choose soft washing if: You need to clean siding, roofing, painted surfaces, or any material that could be damaged by high-pressure water.

Choose both if: You want a complete property cleaning — which is what most Greenville homeowners end up needing. Bundling both services in a single visit saves 10-20% compared to booking them separately.
