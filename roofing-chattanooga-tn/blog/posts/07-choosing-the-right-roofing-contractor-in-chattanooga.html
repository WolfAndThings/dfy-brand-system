Choosing the Right Roofing Contractor in Chattanooga

Your roof is one of the most expensive components of your home. Choosing the wrong contractor means shoddy work, warranty issues, and potentially paying twice. Here is how to find a reliable roofing contractor in Chattanooga and avoid the ones that will cost you more in the long run.

Red Flags to Avoid

  • Storm chasers — out-of-state crews that show up after hailstorms, knock on doors, and offer cheap roofing. They do the work, collect insurance money, and disappear. When problems surface 6 months later, they are unreachable. This is the most common roofing scam in the Chattanooga area.
  • No local address or office — a legitimate Chattanooga roofer has a physical presence in the area, not just a cell phone number.
  • Demanding large upfront deposits — standard practice is no deposit or a small material deposit (10-15%). Anyone asking for 50%+ upfront is a risk.
  • No written contract — verbal agreements leave you with no recourse. A contract should detail materials, timeline, warranty, payment terms, and cleanup expectations.
  • Cash-only payment — reputable contractors accept multiple payment methods and provide receipts.
  • Pressure to sign immediately — "this price is only good today" is a sales tactic, not a legitimate business practice.
  • No insurance certificates — if a worker falls off your roof and the company is uninsured, your homeowner policy may be liable.

What to Look For in a Chattanooga Roofer

  • Tennessee contractor license — verify the license is current and matches the company name on your contract
  • General liability and workers compensation insurance — ask for certificates and verify they are current. Call the insurance company to confirm if needed.
  • Manufacturer certifications — GAF Master Elite, Owens Corning Preferred, or CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster certifications mean the contractor meets higher installation standards and can offer extended manufacturer warranties.
  • Local references and reviews — check Google reviews, BBB rating, and ask for 3-5 recent Chattanooga references you can call.
  • Established local business — how long have they been operating in Chattanooga? Newer companies are not necessarily bad, but a track record matters.
  • Detailed written estimates — the estimate should specify the shingle brand and model, underlayment type, ventilation plan, flashing details, and cleanup process. A one-line quote like "new roof — $8,500" tells you nothing.

Questions to Ask Before Signing

  1. Are you licensed in Tennessee? (Ask for the license number and verify it.)
  2. Can I see your insurance certificates for general liability and workers comp?
  3. How long have you been roofing in the Chattanooga area?
  4. What manufacturer certifications do you hold?
  5. Who will be the project manager on my job, and how do I reach them?
  6. What is your warranty — both workmanship and manufacturer?
  7. Will you handle the building permit?
  8. What happens if you find damage to the decking after tear-off? (Get the per-sheet price in writing.)
  9. What is the payment schedule?
  10. How do you handle cleanup and debris removal?

Understanding Roofing Warranties

There are two types of warranty on every roof job, and both matter:

  • Manufacturer warranty — covers defects in the shingle material itself. Standard is 25-30 years for architectural shingles. Extended warranties (50 years or "lifetime") are available through certified contractors but often have conditions and prorated values.
  • Workmanship warranty — covers the contractor's installation quality. This is typically 5-10 years. If shingles blow off because they were not nailed correctly, this is the warranty that covers it. The contractor's workmanship warranty is only as good as the company's stability — if they go out of business, the warranty is worthless.

How to Compare Bids

Get 3 quotes minimum. When comparing, make sure you are comparing the same scope:

  • Same shingle brand and product line
  • Same underlayment (synthetic vs felt)
  • Same ventilation plan (ridge vent, box vents, powered)
  • Drip edge included or extra?
  • Ice and water shield in valleys and at eaves?
  • Decking replacement price per sheet if damage is found?
  • Dump fees and permit fees included or extra?

The cheapest bid is rarely the best value. If one quote is significantly lower than the others, they are likely cutting corners on materials, labor, or cleanup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I get a roof inspection before getting quotes?

Most reputable contractors include a free inspection as part of the estimate process. However, if you want an unbiased assessment, you can hire an independent home inspector ($150-$300) to evaluate your roof before talking to contractors. This gives you a baseline to compare against what contractors tell you.

How long should a roof replacement take?

A standard residential roof replacement (tear-off and re-roof) takes 1-3 days for most Chattanooga homes. Larger homes, steep pitches, or complex roof lines may take 3-5 days. Weather delays are normal. Be cautious of anyone who says they can do it in half a day — speed often means corners are being cut.

Do I need a building permit for a new roof in Chattanooga?

Yes. Hamilton County requires a building permit for roof replacements. Your contractor should handle the permit application and associated fees. If a contractor suggests skipping the permit, that is a major red flag — unpermitted work can cause problems with insurance claims and home sales.
