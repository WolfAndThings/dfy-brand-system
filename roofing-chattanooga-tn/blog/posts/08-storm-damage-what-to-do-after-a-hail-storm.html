Storm Damage: What to Do After a Hail Storm

Chattanooga sits in a corridor that sees regular severe thunderstorms and hail from spring through fall. When a storm hits, knowing what to do in the first 24-72 hours can be the difference between a smooth insurance claim and a denied one. Here is your step-by-step guide.

Immediate Steps After a Hail Storm

  1. Stay safe first — do not get on your roof to inspect damage. Wet shingles are slippery, and hail-damaged shingles can break under your weight.
  2. Document the storm — note the date, time, and severity. Check local weather reports for official hail size measurements. This information strengthens your insurance claim.
  3. Check for interior leaks — look for water stains on ceilings and walls, drips in the attic, and water around windows. If you find active leaks, place buckets and call for emergency tarping.
  4. Photograph everything from the ground — walk around your property and photograph any visible damage: dented gutters, cracked siding, damaged window screens, dented HVAC units, and any debris. These ground-level signs often correlate with roof damage above.
  5. Do not make permanent repairs yet — temporary measures (tarps, buckets) are fine and expected. But do not replace shingles or make permanent repairs before the insurance adjuster inspects. Permanent repairs before inspection can result in a denied claim.
  6. Call your insurance company — file a claim within 24-48 hours of the storm. Tennessee policies typically require notification within 60 days, but sooner is always better.
  7. Call a local roofing contractor — get a professional inspection before the adjuster visit. A reputable contractor documents damage and can be present during the adjuster's inspection to ensure nothing is missed.

How to Identify Hail Damage on Your Roof

Hail damage is not always obvious, especially from the ground. Here is what a professional looks for:

  • Asphalt shingles — dark spots where granules are knocked off, soft spots that feel like a bruised apple, exposed fiberglass mat, random pattern of hits (not in lines like foot traffic)
  • Metal roofing — dents and dings, cosmetic damage that may not affect function but reduces lifespan
  • Gutters and downspouts — dents along the top edge of gutters are a strong indicator of roof damage above
  • Soft metals around the roof — dented vents, flashing, and chimney caps confirm hail impact at roof level
  • Fence tops and AC units — if these show hail damage, your roof almost certainly has it too

The Insurance Claim Process

  1. File the claim — call your insurance company and provide the storm date, your policy number, and a description of suspected damage.
  2. Get a professional inspection — have a licensed roofing contractor inspect your roof and provide a written damage report with photos.
  3. Adjuster visit — the insurance company sends an adjuster to inspect the damage. Having your contractor present during this visit is important — they can point out damage the adjuster might miss.
  4. Review the claim settlement — the insurance company issues a settlement based on the adjuster's report. Compare it to your contractor's estimate. If the numbers do not match, you can file a supplemental claim.
  5. Choose your contractor — you have the right to choose any licensed contractor, not one the insurance company recommends. Get the work done and submit invoices for the recoverable depreciation portion.

Beware of Storm Chasers

After every major hail event in Chattanooga, out-of-town roofing crews show up going door to door. These storm chasers create problems:

  • They may inflate damage to maximize the insurance payout (and their profit)
  • They often use subcontracted labor with no quality control
  • When warranty issues arise months later, they are gone — back to chasing the next storm in another state
  • Some have been known to cause additional damage during their "free inspection" to create a claim where none existed

Stick with a locally established Chattanooga roofing company that will be here next year and the year after.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a hail damage claim in Tennessee?

Most Tennessee homeowner policies require you to report damage within 60 days, but the statute of limitations for filing the actual claim is typically 1-2 years depending on your policy. File as soon as possible — the longer you wait, the harder it is to prove the damage was storm-related and not normal wear.

Will my insurance rates go up if I file a hail claim?

Hail claims are generally considered "no-fault" — you did not cause the damage. Most Tennessee insurers do not raise rates for a single weather-related claim. However, multiple claims in a short period may affect your rates. Check with your agent before filing for minor damage.

What if the adjuster says there is no damage but my contractor found it?

You have the right to request a re-inspection or file a supplemental claim. Have your contractor provide detailed documentation (photos, measurements, written report) and request that they be present for the second inspection. If the claim is still denied, you can hire a public adjuster or file a complaint with the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance.
