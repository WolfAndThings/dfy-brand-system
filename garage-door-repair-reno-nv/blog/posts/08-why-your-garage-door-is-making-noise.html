Why Your Garage Door Is Making Noise

Grinding, squeaking, or rattling garage door in Reno? Noise is your garage door telling you something needs attention. Here are the most common causes and what to do about each one.

Squeaking or Squealing

Cause: Dry hinges, rollers, or springs. This is the most common noise complaint and the easiest to fix.

Fix: Lubricate all hinges, rollers, and spring coils with white lithium grease or silicone spray. Avoid WD-40 — it dries out quickly and makes the problem worse long-term. In Reno's dry climate, lubrication wears off faster than average. If you haven't lubricated in 6+ months, that is likely the issue.

Cost to fix: $0 (DIY) or $75-$125 for a professional tune-up.

Grinding or Scraping

Cause: Worn rollers, bent tracks, or misaligned door sections.

Fix: Metal rollers grinding in the tracks usually means the rollers need replacement. Nylon rollers are significantly quieter than steel and last 2-3 times longer. If the grinding is localized to one spot, the track may be bent or the door section may be misaligned. Do not force the door open or closed — this makes the problem worse.

Cost to fix: Roller replacement: $125-$200. Track repair: $125-$250. See our roller replacement service.

Rattling or Vibrating

Cause: Loose hardware — bolts, brackets, or opener mounting hardware.

Fix: Tighten all visible bolts with a socket wrench. Check the opener mounting brackets and the bracket that attaches the arm to the door. Also check the chain tension on chain-drive openers — a loose chain slaps against the rail and creates a rattling noise on every cycle.

Cost to fix: $0 (DIY with a socket wrench) or included in a professional tune-up.

Popping or Snapping

Cause: Torsion spring tension adjusting, or a spring that is near the end of its life.

Fix: A single loud pop usually means a spring broke. If the door suddenly feels very heavy or won't open, a broken spring is almost certainly the cause. Do not attempt to open the door manually with a broken spring — the door can weigh 150-400 pounds without spring assistance. Call for spring replacement immediately.

Cost to fix: Spring replacement: $175-$350 for a pair.

Banging When Opening or Closing

Cause: Unbalanced door, worn springs, or opener force set too high.

Fix: An unbalanced door slams at the end of travel because the springs are not properly counterbalancing the weight. Disconnect the opener and test the balance — lift the door halfway and let go. If it drops or shoots up, the springs need adjustment. This is a professional job due to the extreme tension involved.

Cost to fix: Spring adjustment: $100-$175.

Humming or Buzzing (Opener)

Cause: Opener motor running but door not moving, stripped gears, or capacitor failing.

Fix: If the motor hums but the door does not move, the gears inside the opener are likely stripped. This is common on openers older than 10 years. The motor is working, but it cannot transfer power to the drive mechanism. Gear kits can be replaced without buying a whole new opener. See our opener repair page.

Cost to fix: Gear replacement: $150-$250. New opener: $300-$600 installed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a noisy garage door dangerous?

Noise itself is not dangerous, but it signals wear that can lead to failure. A squeaky door just needs lube. A grinding or popping door may have a component close to breaking. The most serious risk is a snapping torsion spring — these release enormous force and can cause injury or property damage. Address unusual noises promptly.

Will switching to nylon rollers make my door quieter?

Yes, dramatically. Nylon rollers reduce noise by 50-75% compared to steel rollers. They also last longer (12-15 years vs 5-7 years for steel) and do not require lubrication. If your neighbors can hear your garage door, nylon rollers are the single best upgrade.
