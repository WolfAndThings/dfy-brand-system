Garage Door Maintenance Checklist

A seasonal garage door maintenance checklist for Reno homeowners. These simple steps take 30 minutes twice a year and can extend the life of your door by 5-10 years while preventing costly emergency repairs.

Monthly: Visual Inspection (2 Minutes)

  • Watch the door open and close completely. It should move smoothly without jerking, hesitating, or making unusual sounds.
  • Check that the door sits flush with the ground when closed — gaps mean the springs or tracks need adjustment.
  • Look at the cables for fraying. If you see any broken strands, do not use the door — call for spring and cable repair immediately.

Every 6 Months: Full Maintenance (30 Minutes)

Lubrication

  • Apply white lithium grease or silicone spray to all moving metal parts: hinges, rollers, spring coils, and the opener chain or screw drive.
  • Do NOT use WD-40 as a lubricant — it is a solvent that cleans but does not lubricate. It dries out and attracts dust.
  • Reno's dry, dusty climate means lubricant wears off faster than in humid areas. Twice-yearly lubrication is the minimum.

Hardware Tightening

  • Check all bracket bolts and roller bracket hardware with a socket wrench. Vibration loosens bolts over time.
  • Tighten the opener mounting brackets — the motor unit vibrates every cycle and fasteners loosen gradually.

Weatherstripping

  • Inspect the bottom seal for cracks, gaps, or missing sections. Reno's UV exposure degrades rubber weatherstripping faster than average.
  • Check side and top seals. Replace any that are brittle, cracked, or no longer making contact with the frame.
  • A good seal keeps out dust, rodents, and weather — all common Reno garage issues.

Safety System Test

  • Auto-reverse test: Place a 2x4 flat on the ground where the door closes. The door should reverse within 2 seconds of touching the board. If it does not, the opener needs adjustment.
  • Photo-eye test: Wave your hand through the beam while the door is closing. It should stop and reverse immediately. Clean the photo-eye lenses with a soft cloth — Reno dust builds up fast.

Balance Test

  • Disconnect the opener by pulling the release handle. Lift the door manually to waist height and let go. It should stay in place or drift only slightly.
  • If the door drops or rises, the springs are out of balance. Do not attempt spring adjustment yourself — springs are under extreme tension and cause serious injuries. Call a professional.

Reno-Specific Maintenance Tips

  • Dust and sand — Reno's desert environment means more dust in the tracks. Wipe tracks with a damp cloth during maintenance. Do not lubricate tracks — it causes rollers to slide instead of roll.
  • UV damage — sun exposure fades and weakens door panels over time. If you have a south or west-facing garage, consider UV-resistant paint or a door with a factory UV coating.
  • Winter prep — before the first freeze, lubricate all moving parts and check weatherstripping. Cold-stiffened rollers and dry springs are the top cause of winter garage door failures in Reno.

When to Call a Pro Instead of DIY

  • Broken or frayed cables
  • Spring replacement (torsion or extension)
  • Door off-track
  • Opener motor not responding
  • Bent or damaged tracks
  • Any issue involving the bottom bracket (under extreme spring tension)

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I lubricate my garage door?

Twice a year minimum in Reno. If your garage door is getting heavy use (4+ cycles per day) or you notice squeaking between maintenance windows, add a mid-season lubrication. It takes 5 minutes and prevents premature wear on rollers, hinges, and springs.

Can I replace garage door rollers myself?

You can replace the non-bottom-bracket rollers yourself — the ones held by hinges along the side of the door. The bottom rollers are connected to the cable system under spring tension and should only be handled by a professional. Nylon rollers are quieter and last longer than steel.
