How to Choose a New Garage Door

Shopping for a new garage door in Reno? With so many materials, styles, and insulation options, making the right choice can feel overwhelming. Here is a practical breakdown to help you decide.

Garage Door Materials Compared

  • Steel — the most popular choice. Durable, low-maintenance, and available in every style. Single-layer steel is the budget option ($800-$1,500 installed). Double or triple-layer with insulation runs $1,200-$3,000+. Steel holds up well in Reno's dry climate but can dent from impact.
  • Wood — traditional look, higher maintenance. Needs repainting or restaining every 2-3 years. Cost: $1,500-$4,000+ installed. Reno's low humidity is actually good for wood doors — less warping and rot than humid climates. Best for custom homes where curb appeal is the priority.
  • Aluminum — lightweight, rust-resistant, modern look. Works well for contemporary homes and wide openings. Cost: $1,200-$2,500 installed. Dents more easily than steel but does not rust — a plus in Reno's occasional wet winters.
  • Fiberglass — lightweight, dent-resistant, won't rust or rot. Cost: $1,000-$2,500 installed. Can crack in extreme cold, but Reno's winters are moderate enough that this is rarely an issue. Good for coastal-style homes.
  • Composite/faux wood — looks like wood, maintains like steel. Best of both worlds for homeowners who want the wood aesthetic without the upkeep. Cost: $1,500-$3,500 installed.

Insulation: Why It Matters in Reno

Reno has hot summers (95F+) and cold winters (20s-30s F). If your garage is attached to your home or you use it as a workshop, insulation directly affects your energy bills and comfort.

  • Non-insulated (single layer): R-value 0. Fine for detached garages used only for parking.
  • Polystyrene insulated: R-value 6-9. Good mid-range option. Keeps garage 10-15 degrees cooler in summer.
  • Polyurethane insulated: R-value 12-18. Best insulation per inch of thickness. Recommended for attached garages and anyone who spends time in the garage. See our insulation guide for more details.

Sizing Your New Door

Standard residential garage door sizes in Reno:

  • Single car: 8x7 or 9x7 feet
  • Double car: 16x7 feet
  • RV/oversize: 10x8 or 12x8 feet

Older Reno homes sometimes have non-standard openings. We measure before ordering to make sure the door fits perfectly — a mis-sized door causes seal gaps, hardware stress, and premature wear.

Cost Breakdown: New Garage Door in Reno

  • Budget steel door (non-insulated): $800-$1,200 installed
  • Mid-range insulated steel: $1,200-$2,200 installed
  • Premium insulated with windows: $2,200-$3,500 installed
  • Custom wood or carriage-style: $3,000-$6,000+ installed

Installation includes removing and disposing of the old door, installing new tracks and hardware, and adjusting springs and opener. Most installations take 3-5 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a new garage door last?

Steel and aluminum doors last 20-30 years with minimal maintenance. Wood doors last 15-20 years with regular refinishing. The springs and opener will need replacement before the door itself — springs every 7-12 years, openers every 10-15 years.

Can I keep my existing opener with a new door?

Usually yes, as long as the opener can handle the weight of the new door. Insulated doors are heavier than non-insulated, so if you are upgrading from a single-layer to a triple-layer door, we check the opener capacity. If it is undersized, we recommend upgrading at the same time — bundling saves on labor costs.

Does a new garage door increase home value?

Yes. A new garage door replacement consistently ranks as one of the highest ROI home improvements — typically recovering 90-100% of the cost at resale. For Reno homes where the garage door is a major part of the front facade, the curb appeal impact is significant.
