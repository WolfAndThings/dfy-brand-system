Retaining Wall Options for Sloped Yards

Got a sloped yard in Des Moines? A retaining wall prevents erosion, creates usable flat space, and can add significant value to your property. Here is a comparison of the most common retaining wall options for Iowa homeowners.

Concrete Block Retaining Walls

Interlocking concrete blocks (like Allan Block or Versa-Lok) are the most popular choice for Des Moines residential retaining walls.

  • Cost: $20-$35 per square face foot installed
  • Height limit: Up to 4 feet without engineering; taller walls need a geotechnical engineer's design
  • Pros: Wide variety of colors and textures, no mortar required, good drainage through the joints, DIY-friendly for short walls
  • Cons: Not ideal for walls over 6 feet, requires proper backfill and drainage or they lean and fail
  • Lifespan: 50+ years with proper drainage

Poured Concrete Retaining Walls

Solid poured concrete is the strongest option and handles the heaviest loads.

  • Cost: $30-$60 per square face foot installed
  • Height limit: Can be engineered for any height
  • Pros: Strongest option, longest lifespan, can be formed in curves, accepts decorative finishes (stamping, staining)
  • Cons: Higher cost, requires forms and more labor, needs proper drainage behind the wall, cannot be easily modified later
  • Lifespan: 75-100+ years

Natural Stone Retaining Walls

Stacked natural stone (limestone, fieldstone, or boulders) offers a more organic look.

  • Cost: $25-$50 per square face foot installed
  • Height limit: Typically 3-4 feet for dry-stacked; taller with mortar
  • Pros: Natural appearance, blends with landscaping, excellent drainage through natural gaps
  • Cons: Labor-intensive, heavier material to work with, irregular shapes make tall walls tricky
  • Lifespan: 50+ years for mortared; dry-stacked may need periodic resetting

Iowa-Specific Considerations for Retaining Walls

  • Frost depth — footings must go below 42 inches (Iowa frost line) or the wall will heave and crack
  • Clay soil drainage — Des Moines clay soil holds water. Every retaining wall needs gravel backfill and a perforated drain pipe behind it. Without drainage, hydrostatic pressure will push the wall over
  • Permits — Des Moines requires a building permit for retaining walls over 4 feet tall. Walls near property lines may also need a survey and setback verification
  • Freeze-thaw — choose materials rated for Iowa's freeze-thaw cycles. Not all decorative blocks are rated for below-grade exposure in cold climates

Cost Comparison for a Typical Des Moines Project

For a 40-foot-long, 3-foot-tall retaining wall (120 square face feet):

  • Concrete block: $2,400-$4,200
  • Poured concrete: $3,600-$7,200
  • Natural stone: $3,000-$6,000

These ranges include materials, labor, drainage, and backfill. Site access, slope severity, and soil conditions can push costs higher.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall in Des Moines?

Walls under 4 feet generally do not require a permit. Walls 4 feet or taller need a building permit and may require engineered drawings. If the wall is near a property line or in a floodplain, additional approvals may be needed. Check with Des Moines Development Services before starting.

How long does it take to build a retaining wall?

A typical residential wall (30-50 linear feet, 3-4 feet tall) takes 2-5 days from excavation to completion. Poured concrete walls require additional time for form setup and curing. Weather delays are common in spring — rain can halt excavation on Iowa's clay soil.
