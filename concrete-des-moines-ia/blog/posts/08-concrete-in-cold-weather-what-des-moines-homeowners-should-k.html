Concrete in Cold Weather: What Des Moines Homeowners Should Know

Pouring concrete in cold weather requires special precautions. In Des Moines, where winter temperatures regularly drop below freezing from November through March, understanding cold-weather concrete is essential for any project.

Why Cold Weather Is a Problem for Concrete

Concrete cures through a chemical reaction (hydration) that generates heat. When air temperatures drop below 40 degrees F, this reaction slows dramatically. Below 25 degrees F, it can stop entirely. If fresh concrete freezes before reaching a compressive strength of 500 PSI (usually within the first 24-48 hours), the ice crystals permanently damage the internal structure. The result: a weak slab that cracks, flakes, and crumbles prematurely.

Des Moines Cold Weather Concrete Season

In Des Moines, cold weather concrete precautions typically apply from mid-November through mid-March. Average temperatures during these months:

  • November: Highs 45-50F, Lows 28-32F — borderline, precautions recommended
  • December-February: Highs 28-35F, Lows 12-18F — full cold weather protocols required
  • March: Highs 45-55F, Lows 25-33F — precautions still needed for early-month pours

How Professionals Handle Cold Weather Pours

  • Hot water in the mix — concrete plants heat the mix water to deliver concrete at 50-65 degrees F on arrival
  • Accelerators — calcium chloride or non-chloride accelerators speed up the set time so concrete gains strength faster
  • Insulated blankets — placed over fresh concrete immediately after finishing. These trap the heat of hydration and keep the slab above freezing during the critical first 48-72 hours
  • Ground thawing — frozen ground must be thawed before pouring. Pouring on frozen subgrade causes the slab to settle and crack when the ground thaws in spring
  • Heated enclosures — for foundation walls and critical pours, temporary enclosures with propane heaters maintain temperature around the concrete

When to Delay Your Pour

Even with precautions, some conditions make it better to wait:

  • Sustained temperatures below 20 degrees F for 48+ hours after the pour
  • Heavy snow or ice storm forecast within 24 hours
  • Wind chill creating rapid heat loss from the concrete surface
  • Frozen ground that cannot be adequately thawed

A delayed pour costs you time. A failed cold-weather pour costs you the entire slab plus removal and replacement — easily 2-3x the original cost.

Freeze-Thaw Damage on Existing Concrete

Des Moines sees 50-70 freeze-thaw cycles per year. Each cycle forces water in the concrete to expand and contract, gradually breaking down the surface. Signs of freeze-thaw damage include:

  • Spalling — the surface flakes off in thin layers
  • Scaling — the top layer peels away, exposing aggregate
  • Pop-outs — small cone-shaped holes where aggregate near the surface froze and broke out

The best defense: apply a penetrating concrete sealer every 2-3 years. This keeps water from saturating the concrete in the first place. See our driveway maintenance guide for sealer recommendations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I pour a driveway in Des Moines in November?

Yes, with proper cold-weather precautions. Early November is usually fine — temperatures are still moderate. Late November gets riskier. Your contractor should monitor the 10-day forecast and plan accordingly. Expect a small surcharge (10-15%) for cold-weather materials and blankets.

Does road salt damage my concrete driveway?

Yes. Salt (sodium chloride) and especially magnesium chloride deicers accelerate freeze-thaw damage. New concrete should not be exposed to deicing chemicals for the first winter. After that, use sand for traction instead of salt when possible, and apply a sealer annually to reduce salt penetration.

What is the best time of year to pour concrete in Des Moines?

April through October. Ideal pouring conditions are 50-80 degrees F with moderate humidity. Spring is the busiest season for concrete contractors in Des Moines, so scheduling in late summer or early fall often means faster availability and the same quality results.
