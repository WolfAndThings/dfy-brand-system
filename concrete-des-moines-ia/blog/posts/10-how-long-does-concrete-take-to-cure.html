How Long Does Concrete Take to Cure

How long does concrete take to cure in Des Moines? The short answer: 28 days for full strength. But there are important milestones along the way that determine when you can walk on it, drive on it, and put heavy loads on it.

The 28-Day Curing Timeline

  • 24 hours: Initial set. Concrete is hard enough for light foot traffic. Do not drag furniture or heavy items across it.
  • 48 hours: Safe for normal foot traffic. Forms can usually be removed from vertical pours.
  • 7 days: Concrete has reached roughly 70% of its final strength. Light vehicles (passenger cars) can usually drive on driveways at this point.
  • 14 days: Approximately 85-90% strength. Most normal use is fine.
  • 28 days: Full design strength achieved (typically 3,000-4,000 PSI for residential). Heavy vehicles, dumpsters, and construction equipment are safe.

What Affects Curing Time in Des Moines

Several factors specific to Iowa can speed up or slow down the curing process:

  • Temperature — ideal curing happens at 50-75 degrees F. Des Moines summers (highs 85-95F) can cause concrete to cure too fast on the surface, leading to cracking. Winter temps below 40F slow curing dramatically and require insulated blankets.
  • Humidity — concrete needs moisture to cure. Iowa's humid summers help. Dry, windy spring days can pull moisture from the surface too quickly.
  • Mix design — higher-strength mixes (4,000+ PSI) take the same 28 days but reach usable strength faster. Accelerators can reduce early-stage cure times by 30-50%.
  • Thickness — a 4-inch sidewalk cures faster throughout than an 8-inch foundation wall. Thicker pours retain more internal heat, which helps in winter but can cause thermal cracking in summer.

How to Help Concrete Cure Properly

  • Keep it wet — mist the surface with water 2-3 times daily for the first 7 days, or apply a curing compound immediately after finishing. This is the single most important thing you can do.
  • Do not seal too early — wait at least 28 days before applying any sealer. Sealing too early traps moisture and can cause whitening or peeling.
  • Avoid deicers the first winter — new concrete should not be exposed to salt or chemical deicers for its first full winter. Use sand for traction instead.
  • No heavy loads early — keep construction equipment, dumpsters, and moving trucks off new concrete for the full 28 days.

Signs of Poor Curing

If concrete was not cured properly, you will see these problems within the first year:

  • Surface dusting — a powdery residue that never stops appearing when you sweep
  • Crazing — a network of fine hairline cracks across the surface (looks like a dry lake bed)
  • Scaling — the top layer peels away, especially after the first freeze-thaw cycle
  • Low strength — the concrete chips or gouges easily under normal use

If you see these signs, the damage is permanent. The affected concrete may need resurfacing or replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I park on my new driveway after 3 days?

We recommend waiting 7 days for passenger vehicles and 28 days for heavy trucks or SUVs. At 3 days, the concrete is only at about 50% strength. Parking a vehicle will not cause it to crack, but turning your wheels while stationary (called "power steering marks") can scuff the surface permanently at this stage.

Does concrete keep getting stronger after 28 days?

Yes, technically. Concrete continues to hydrate and gain strength for years, but the rate slows dramatically after 28 days. At one year, concrete is typically 10-15% stronger than at 28 days. For practical purposes, 28-day strength is the design standard.

Should I water my new concrete in summer?

Yes. In Des Moines summer heat, misting your new concrete 2-3 times per day for the first week prevents the surface from drying too fast and cracking. Early morning and late afternoon are the best times. Alternatively, your contractor can apply a liquid curing compound that locks in moisture.
